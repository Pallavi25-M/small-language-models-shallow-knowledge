Dresden ( DREZ-den; German: [ˈdʁeːsdn̩] ; Upper Saxon: Dräsdn; Upper Sorbian: Drježdźany, pronounced [ˈdʁʲɛʒdʒanɨ]) is the capital city of the German state of Saxony and its second most populous city after Leipzig. It is the 12th most populous city of Germany, and the third-most populous city in the area of former East Germany, after Berlin and Leipzig. Located on the Elbe River in the Dresden Basin, the city forms part of an urban area that includes the towns of Freital, Pirna, Radebeul, Coswig, Radeberg, and Heidenau with a combined population of over 780,000. The Dresden metropolitan area has over 1.3 million inhabitants.
First documented in 1206, Dresden developed into the principal residence of the Wettin rulers of Saxony from the late 15th century and later served as the capital of the Electorate of Saxony and the Kingdom of Saxony. The Electors and Kings of Saxony for centuries furnished the city with cultural and artistic splendor. The city was known as the Jewel Box, because of its Baroque and Rococo city centre. Following the abolition of the Saxon monarchy in 1918, Dresden remained the state capital. The controversial American and British bombing of Dresden towards the end of World War II killed approximately 25,000 people, most of whom were civilians, and destroyed large parts of the city centre.
After World War II, Dresden became part of the German Democratic Republic (East Germany), where it remained an industrial and research centre. Since German reunification in 1990, Dresden has once again become a cultural, educational and political centre of Germany. Extensive parts of the historic centre have been reconstructed. The Dresden University of Technology (TU Dresden) is one of the 10 largest universities in Germany and part of the German Universities Excellence Initiative. The economy of Dresden and its agglomeration is one of the most dynamic in Germany and ranks first in Saxony. It is dominated by high-tech branches, often called "Silicon Saxony".
Dresden is one of the most visited cities in Germany with 4.7 million overnight stays per year. Its most prominent building is the Frauenkirche located at the Neumarkt. Built in the 18th century, the church was destroyed during World War II. The remaining ruins were left for 50 years as a war memorial, before being rebuilt between 1994 and 2005. Other famous landmarks include the Zwinger, the Semperoper and Dresden Castle. Furthermore, the city is home to the Dresden State Art Collections, originating from the collections of the Saxon electors in the 16th century. Dresden's Striezelmarkt is one of the largest Christmas markets in Germany and is considered the first genuine Christmas market in the world. Nearby sights include the National Park of Saxon Switzerland, the Ore Mountains and the countryside around Elbe Valley, Moritzburg Castle and Meissen, home of Meissen porcelain.
